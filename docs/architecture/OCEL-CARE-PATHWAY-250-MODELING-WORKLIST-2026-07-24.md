**Purpose:** One-row-per-pathway technical normalization and measurement backlog. This is not clinical approval, a coding directive, an order set, or a claim that any candidate terminology is appropriate for a specific patient.

## How to read this worklist

- **Lane** is copied from the governed release disposition: 96 pathways can enter institutional-signoff normalization, 148 need specialist review with documented limitations first, and 6 need redesign or explicit non-protocol classification.
- **Complexity** is a technical workload heuristic derived from narrative clause volume, timing language, conditional language, and surgical workflow—not clinical severity, risk, or activation readiness.
- **Clause / time / condition counts** are lexical inventory aids. They identify text that a knowledge engineer must decompose; they are not executable steps or validated rules.
- **Terminology counts** reproduce the workbook's source-verified candidate counts. They prove source existence only. Descendant/category fan-out, inherited mappings, and lexical matches must not be treated as clinical equivalence.
- **Object focus** is the minimum object-centric scope to investigate. Each pathway still needs site-specific source bindings, identity rules, timestamp semantics, exception handling, and clinician approval.
- Every pathway must be measured using the common architecture in the companion research report: applicability, version pinning, observed-event binding, step/constraint ledger, coverage/confidence, outcome and safety screens, and human review.

## Required exit evidence for every row

A pathway leaves this worklist only when all of the following are versioned and reviewable:

1. Institutional scope, accountable owner, and approval period.
2. Patient/encounter applicability logic, explicit exclusions, and clinician-confirmation policy.
3. Stable pathway stages, atomic activities, milestones, goals, branches, alternatives, repeats, and exception states.
4. Temporal constraints with named anchors, lower/upper bounds, units, calendar semantics, and early/late interpretation.
5. Source bindings for each expected observation, including code system/version, local-code mappings, FHIR/source fields, timestamp role, object links, correction/retraction behavior, and provenance.
6. Resource requirements and queue-state events sufficient to distinguish waiting, processing, blocked, canceled, and unavailable states.
7. Safety/outcome measures with numerator, denominator, exclusions, risk adjustment or stratification, and harm/adjudication rules.
8. Synthetic tests, chart-reviewed gold cases, inter-rater agreement, data-completeness thresholds, and shadow-mode performance.
9. Immutable model digest, evaluator version, source cutoff, measurement run ID, and reproducible patient-step evidence.
10. A governed projection policy for staff, patient, Hummingbird, 4D, Arena, and Eddy surfaces; raw research prose is never exposed directly.
